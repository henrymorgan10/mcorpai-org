The arithmetic

Most aid impact is estimated. This one is calculated.

Film is the rare aid instrument that is audited by default. We do not count the admissions; the national ticketing system counts them. The break-even point follows from a published budget. So the amount transferred is not a claim we make. It is a result anyone can reproduce.

G = A × N × r
Gamount transferred
Aadmissions, externally audited
Nnet revenue per admission
rgiving ratio, published in advance

Every term on the right is externally audited or published in advance.
The left side cannot be inflated in a report.

Net revenue per admission depends on cinema and distribution terms and is disclosed with each settlement. For a community screening there is no cinema or distributor share, so the net equals the ticket price.

A note on terminology

Under OECD DAC rules, ODA means official flows from official agencies. This project is a private flow and does not describe itself as ODA. It is built so that its outputs can be reported in ODA-compatible units, and so that a public agency could adopt it as an implementing modality without redesigning it.

01 — Why this exists

The money is already being spent.
What's missing is the record.

A design problem, not a goodwill problem

Corporate giving runs into the billions every year. Organisations can say how much they spent. Very few can say who changed, and how. What is missing is structure, not sincerity.

Enjoying and giving sit too far apart

A film is a film; a donation is a separate decision made on a separate day. The wider that gap, the fewer people cross it. This project closes the gap.

The people who make films cannot hold on

Crew income arrives per production. When rent and medical bills pile up between projects, skilled people leave — and most never come back. Protecting them protects the capacity to make anything at all.

02 — How it works

Four cuts. That's the whole thing.

CUT 1

Make it

Small budget, high craft. The less there is to recover, the sooner the giving starts.

CUT 2

Show it

Cinemas, university halls, community centres, company auditoriums, a lawn by the river. A white wall is a cinema.

CUT 3

Split it

The producer's share is divided by a rule fixed in advance. A rule decided after the money arrives is not a promise.

CUT 4

Publish it

Funds go straight to hospitals and support bodies in that region, and every figure is published.

03 — How the money moves

"If the film flops, there's no donation — right?"

Wrong. Giving starts with the very first ticket.

Before the budget is recovered
50% given
50% recovery
After the budget is recovered
100% of what remains to the producer is given
If it is never recovered
Nothing already given is taken back. The loss is the producer's.

This is not a structure where giving waits for success. Succeed and more people are helped; fail and I take the loss. That is all.

That is why the budget stays small

A large commercial film has so much to recover that giving is pushed far into the future. A budget-controlled art film recovers quickly, so the giving starts quickly. The baseline is a production budget under USD 1.2 million, extending to USD 2.4 million at most. Anything beyond that is considered only when several partners build it together.

These figures are planning baselines, not confirmed amounts. Actual settlement depends on cinema and distribution terms. Read them as the ceiling I intend to hold.

Why film, and why a small one

A hundred-million-dollar film cannot be an aid instrument. A one-point-two-million-dollar film can.

The larger the sum that must be recovered, the further into the future the giving is pushed, often past the point where it would ever begin. Below roughly 1.2 million dollars, recovery is fast enough that transfers start in the first weeks of release. This is not a claim about any one country. It is a condition that any mid-budget film industry in the world can meet.

Money spent disappears.
Trust compounds.

04 — Five commitments

No exception, at any scale.

  1. The splitting rule is published before the screening and never changed afterwards in our favour.
  2. No operating fee is deducted from what is given. It passes through whole.
  3. Money raised in a place goes to that place's hospitals and support bodies.
  4. Nobody's suffering is used as fundraising material.
  5. No link to any party, movement or political camp. Ever.
05 — The line we will not cross

We will not show you a sick child's face.

Showing a sick child raises money. That is simply true. We are not going to do it.

Nothing has been placed here.

Intentionally left empty

Children grow up.
When that photograph was taken, the child had no power to refuse.

One day, healthy and grown, they may find the worst moment of their life still sitting on the internet. That can become a second injury. It can follow them through school.

Medical records, and images made with the full understanding and consent of the person and their guardian, are a different matter. What we will not do is take a photograph in order to raise money.

Then how do you move people?

By showing them a beautiful film.

Give an audience feeling instead of guilt. Let them share art instead of consuming someone's misfortune. Then send the result of that enjoyment back to the children. We want to prove that dignity and scale are not in conflict.

06 — Screenings

A film should be watchable anywhere.

Multiplexes are not the only cinemas. Independent houses, university halls, community centres, company auditoriums, libraries, a lawn by the river. One projector and a white wall is enough.

Does it matter whether the ticket is three dollars or ten?

Pay one dollar if money is tight. Pay twenty if you want to do more. Making sure nobody is priced out of culture matters more than maximising the total.

In a university or a town, it works like this

A thousand people in a hall at ten dollars each raises ten thousand dollars. We do not decide where it goes. The students and the institution choose a hospital or support body in their own area.

The purpose stays fixed; the recipient is chosen by whoever opens the doors. People need to be able to see, with their own eyes, where their money went in their own town.

07 — The people who make it

Film crews have to survive too.

Part of the proceeds supports crew members and emerging creators. Priority goes to those whose rent has fallen behind, or who are ill and not seeing a doctor.

  • No cash, no vouchers. Rent and medical bills only, paid directly.
  • Around USD 400 a month, up to USD 600.
  • Three months minimum, twelve months maximum.
  • I do not decide alone. A review panel with an external majority decides.
  • Support is limited to funds already secured. We never promise money we do not have.

This is not welfare.
It is holding on to one person so they do not quit.

08 — Alongside those who are ill for a long time

The more hands the money passes through,
the less arrives and the blurrier the responsibility.

01

Seriously ill children

Funds are placed directly with partner university and specialist hospitals under written agreement — no intermediary body in between.

02

Chronic pain conditions

Treatment costs continue while the pain itself makes regular work impossible. Illness creates the need for money and removes the means to earn it.

03

Neighbours nearby

Free meal services, food for children heading households, free clinics for the medically underserved. Faith-based or not — we look only at delivery and accounting.

For chronic pain, two things are prepared together

WhatHow
TreatmentPaid from funds already placed with the partner hospital. No cash is sent to personal accounts.
WorkPacking, checking and record work that can be done from home is assigned first. Terms and pay are set by a formal employment contract.
ResearchA share of the fund goes to treatment research. Results are held jointly with the hospital, and any revenue returns to the fund.

People who developed such conditions during national service, and lost access to care afterwards, are included as a priority group. This is not a judgement on any policy. There is a gap, and we are filling it.

09 — The Sharing Card

A collectible card made alongside the film

  • Only characters we create ourselves. We do not borrow anyone else's intellectual property.
  • Printed on recycled stock to keep the environmental load down.
  • Packing is shared between social enterprises employing disabled workers, public-work programmes for older and homeless people, and home-based work for those whose pain makes commuting impossible.
  • Limited editions may go to charity auction, with the split published before issue.

The sharing fund is split again between crew support and treatment costs; the exact ratio is published at issue. The card is attached only to works whose proceeds go entirely to public benefit. If it becomes merchandise that simply sells well, its reason to exist disappears.

10 — For organisations

No contract lands on your desk first.
Pick one of three.

OPTION 1

Back the production

Cover part of the budget with funds you do not expect back. Several partners sharing the load can carry a large work with no recovery pressure on anyone.

OPTION 2

Screen it in-house

Hold a charity screening in your own hall. Ticket money from your staff goes to a hospital or support body your staff choose.

OPTION 3

Give goods or skills

Equipment, printing, logistics, venues, platforms. It does not have to be money.

You get numbers, not the phrase "we donated"

How many took part, how much was raised, which institution received it, what it paid for, and who verified it. A report (PDF) and the raw data (CSV) come together. Making it usable in your own internal reporting is our job, not yours.

Reporting standards differ between organisations, so how this appears in your own disclosures is something we agree with you rather than assume.

11 — Across borders

Not giving something. Leaving something behind.

For these children to go to school instead of begging,
what has to remain in the village?

Aid that is handed out ends the moment the handing out stops. So we do not give content. We leave behind a structure in which content generates income locally.

01Screening rights are granted to the community at no cost.
02A low-cost projector and screen that can be repaired locally comes with it.
03The community sets its own ticket price and runs its own small cinema.
04All revenue stays with the community. We take nothing.
05Residents sell their own drinks and snacks for additional income.
06Repair training is included so nobody depends on outside technicians.

What we will never do

  • Use a region's poverty as promotional imagery.
  • Frame any country or culture as lesser than another.
  • Film or publish anything without the local partner's consent.
  • Promise a scale we cannot sustain.

Equipment that cannot be repaired
is not aid. It is a liability.

Alignment

Where the outputs land

  • 3.8 — Universal health coverage. Treatment costs paid directly to partner hospitals, with no intermediary body.
  • 4.1 — Access to education. Screening income is retained by the host community, including for schooling.
  • 8.5 — Decent work. Formal contracts for home-based work; packing through social enterprises.
  • 10.2 — Inclusion. Priority for people with chronic pain conditions, disabled workers, and older or homeless workers.
  • 17.16 — Partnership. The method is published under an open licence, free to copy, with no licensing body.
ESG without a gatekeeper

We do not certify anyone. We publish the formula.

Certification takes time, money and staff. For many organisations, particularly outside high-income markets, that cost is itself the barrier — and the standard is often written a long way from where the work is done.

The absence of an assessor is the design goal,
not a gap in the design.

So this project issues no marks and grades nobody. It publishes the formula, the raw ledger and the assumptions behind both. Anyone can compute the same figures, and anyone can dispute ours. Five numbers are enough: amount raised, amount transferred, days taken to transfer, share retained for administration, and share of decisions made locally.

Governance

No single person can move the money.

01

Funds never rest with us

Money is placed directly with partner hospitals and support bodies under written agreement. The project does not hold beneficiary funds at any point.

02

An external majority decides

Recipients of support are selected by a review panel in which external members hold the majority. The proposer and any related party is excluded from every category.

03

Cooperative operation

Screening operations, packing work and crew support run through a cooperative structure, so that employment terms and accounts are visible to the people inside them.

What this project values

What a collaborator can expect beyond a fee.

This project grew out of a working culture in which sharing is treated as a form of competence rather than an ornament placed on top of it. That shapes what we can offer a partner, and what we will not ask of one.

  1. Credit is named, not absorbed. Contributors appear by name in the ledger and in the credits.
  2. Disagreement is recorded. Where a partner objected and we proceeded anyway, the objection is published beside the decision.
  3. No exclusivity is asked for. Nobody is required to stop working with anyone else.
  4. Slow is acceptable. A partner who needs six months for internal approval has not refused.
13 — What we do not claim

Nothing undecided will be described here as decided.

It is the fastest way for a public-interest project to lose trust. So, plainly:

  • No actor, director or organisation has confirmed participation yet.
  • Festival selection is not ours to decide. The work and the jury decide it.
  • A ticket is not a donation. A ticket price is not tax-deductible. Where a separate contribution is made through a body that meets the legal requirements in your jurisdiction, that body may issue a receipt.
  • We do not claim this project cures a disease or single-handedly rescues an industry.

What can be promised is not the size of the result,
but keeping the rule and publishing the numbers.

14 — Finally

I would rather not say that one film changes the world. That cannot be proven, and what cannot be proven does not last.

This can be proven: where the money from one screening went, how many people it reached, and how those people are doing six months later.

If the rule survives,
the work continues even when the people change.

If the film fails, I will keep going. What I want to leave behind is not a large result but a rule anyone can copy exactly. The day this runs without me is the day it has actually worked.

Watch a good film; do a good thing at the same time. Building that small connection, over and over, is the whole of this project. My sincere thanks to everyone who takes part.
