What matters in this model is not how sophisticated or luxurious the cinema facilities are. The core value offered to international visitors is not the experience of watching a film in a high-end cinema, but rather **the experience of watching films alongside local residents, interacting with the community, and knowing that even their modest spending directly contributes to local residents’ income, children’s education, and ultimately the community’s self-reliance.**

Admission prices can also be differentiated based on local economic conditions and the ability of international visitors to pay. This model can also be connected to **Community-Based Accommodation** for international visitors who wish to stay for several days to watch multiple films. Rather than having accommodation operated by outside businesses that take the profits out of the community, the model is designed so that **local NGOs and community organizations oversee basic standards for safety, hygiene, and financial transparency, while trained local residents directly participate in day-to-day operations such as accommodation management, cleaning, laundry, and meal preparation.**

For example, if basic accommodation that meets appropriate safety and hygiene standards is offered for approximately **$30 per night**, the model can be structured so that a substantial portion of the accommodation revenue circulates back into **wages and income for local residents involved in the operation, facility maintenance, and a community fund**, rather than flowing out to external businesses. Specific revenue-sharing arrangements and operating procedures should be transparently determined and disclosed through consultation with local NGOs and community representatives.

Therefore, the economic structure of Micro Cinema goes far beyond simply operating a small movie theater.

**$3 Unlimited Movie Access → International Visitors → Longer Stays → $3–4 Spending on Local Food and Beverages → Community-Operated Accommodation and Local Services → Jobs and Real Income for Local Residents → Children’s Education and Healthcare → Community Self-Reliance**

In this model, film serves as **a cultural catalyst that connects visitors with the local community**, while local residents become, as much as possible, **the primary participants in the economic activities** surrounding food, beverages, accommodation, and other local services. Local NGOs and community organizations are not intended to operate these businesses on behalf of residents; rather, their role is to support training, management, accountability, and transparency so that local residents can operate them sustainably themselves.

Ultimately, Micro Cinema aims to become **an ESG-driven self-reliance model that does not turn poverty itself into a tourism product, but instead uses film and culture as the attraction and transforms visitor spending into jobs and direct income for local residents.** Visitors gain a meaningful cultural experience, while local residents participate directly in economic activity and generate their own income. The ultimate goal is to create a virtuous cycle in which these economic benefits support children’s education and healthcare and contribute to the community’s long-term self-reliance.

The core objective is to create this virtuous cycle. If the model can demonstrate tangible results at Manila North Cemetery, it can eventually be developed into **a scalable, ESG-driven model for cultural and educational self-reliance that can be replicated in other impoverished communities.**

**The core purpose of Micro Cinema is to help children in impoverished communities gain access to education and healthcare.**

## Micro Cinema: ESG Through Self-Reliance for Vulnerable Communities

The economic impact of Micro Cinema should not be evaluated solely based on **revenue from the $3 movie admission fee.** Film screenings can create various additional sources of income within the local community, including the sale of handmade snacks, beverages, and souvenirs made directly by local residents. Further economic benefits can also be expected if Micro Cinema is eventually connected with local tourism and cultural experiences.

Because these additional sources of income may vary depending on the number of visitors, local operating conditions, and the level of community participation, it is difficult to calculate the exact amount of revenue in advance. However, this does not mean that all of the social outcomes created by Micro Cinema are difficult to measure.

In particular, education-related outcomes—such as **“How many children from impoverished families received tuition and educational support through Micro Cinema?”, “How many children were able to attend school instead of depending on begging for survival?”, and “How many children were able to remain in school and continue their education?”**—can be tracked and quantified with a relatively high degree of accuracy in cooperation with local NGOs and communities.

Therefore, the ESG performance of Micro Cinema can be measured through specific **Social Impact indicators, including the number of children receiving educational support, school attendance and education retention rates, revenue reinvested into the local community, increases in residents’ income, and jobs created.**

If this data is collected and verified each year using consistent measurement standards, the social value actually created by Micro Cinema can be quantitatively presented and compared on a year-by-year basis. Based on this data, it becomes possible to **produce measurable and verifiable ESG Impact Reports and develop the results into performance data that can be used within international ESG evaluation and disclosure frameworks.**

Ultimately, one of the most important ESG values of Micro Cinema lies in its ability to **demonstrate through data how many children’s lives and educational opportunities have actually been changed through local economic activity.**

## Micro Cinema International Operating Standards to Prevent the Commodification of Poverty

**Poverty must never become a tourism product.** For Micro Cinema to expand across different countries and impoverished communities, it is not enough simply to establish small cinemas under the same name. Wherever Micro Cinema operates, it must uphold a common principle: **poverty should never be used as a tourism resource, and the dignity of local residents must be protected while supporting their self-reliance through film and culture.**

To ensure that this principle is clearly upheld, it is necessary to establish **Micro Cinema International Operating Standards.** These standards are not intended to standardize the physical facilities or appearance of the cinemas. Rather, they are designed to establish common ethical and operational principles for **revenue distribution, pricing policies, community participation, children’s education and healthcare support, fund management, and financial transparency.**

For example, Micro Cinema could establish formal agreements in advance with local NGOs and community representatives and adopt a basic principle under which **30% of distributable revenue is used by the NGO to support the sustainable operation of the project, including management, training, accounting, and impact verification, while 70% is returned to local residents and the local community.** This structure can help prevent outside organizations from taking the majority of the revenue and ensure that as much of the economic value generated by Micro Cinema as possible continues to circulate within the local community.

Pricing policies also require consistent standards. For example, international visitors could be offered **access to available films for a fixed period for $3, while handmade snacks and beverages prepared by local residents could be priced at approximately $3–4, and basic community-based accommodation at approximately $30.** Establishing common pricing guidelines can help prevent visitors from being charged excessive prices simply because they are visiting an impoverished community, while also preventing uncontrolled commercialization from one location to another and preserving the social purpose of Micro Cinema.

However, the core purpose of international standards is not simply to charge exactly the same prices in every country. More importantly, Micro Cinema should **clearly disclose price limits and operating principles and ensure transparency regarding how much revenue is returned to local residents and how community funds are used.** Where local living costs and economic conditions differ significantly, prices may be adjusted in consultation with local NGOs and community representatives. However, the reasons and standards for such adjustments should be publicly disclosed in order to prevent arbitrary pricing and the commercialization of poverty.

Funds returned to the local community should also be managed according to clear priorities. Under the transparent management of local NGOs and with the participation of community representatives, **the highest priority should be given to providing tuition and educational support so that children from impoverished families can remain in school, as well as healthcare support so that they can receive necessary medical care.**

Rather than spending all generated revenue in the short term, a portion should also be reserved in a **Community Future Fund** so that education and healthcare support can continue to benefit not only children today but also future generations. In this way, Micro Cinema can develop beyond a one-time charitable project into **a sustainable community self-reliance structure capable of supporting generations to come.**

Above all, these international operating standards should serve not merely as a business manual for expanding Micro Cinema, but as **an ethical boundary.** Practices that showcase the poverty of local residents to visitors or sell poverty itself as an experience should not be permitted. What visitors experience and spend their money on should instead be **the cultural and economic value created through the direct participation of local residents, including art films, food, cultural experiences, accommodation, and other community-based services.**

**Poverty must not become a tourism product. Culture should be the reason people visit.**

Therefore, what the international standardization of Micro Cinema ultimately seeks to protect is not the price itself, but the fundamental principles of **human dignity, fair revenue distribution, transparent fund management, children’s education and healthcare, and the long-term self-reliance of local communities.**

**Establishing international standards for this cultural model can help preserve clear ethical boundaries while enabling local communities to generate their own income and build self-reliance within those boundaries. This is the fundamental reason why Micro Cinema needs international operating standards.**
